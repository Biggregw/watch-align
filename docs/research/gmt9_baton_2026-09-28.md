# 9 o'clock baton (2026-09-28, alpha59)

## How it works

The 9 uses the same code as the 6 (`GmtSixLandmarkAnalyzer`, now with a `Position`):

- The photo is turned 90° clockwise, which puts the 9 at the top. That is an exact
  pixel move with no resampling, like the 6's 180° turn.
- The 12 marker's tick finder and edge fitting then run unchanged, as they do for the 6.
- The results are mapped back onto the original photo.

**What it measures:**
- **Centring:** the baton's offset from the midpoint of the 44 and 46 tick ends, as a
  fraction of its width. Positive means towards 44, which is lower on the dial.
- **Rotation:** the baton's axis against the square to the 44–46 chord. Positive means
  clockwise.

**What it shares with the 6:**
- Every gate: the orientation check (the 9 must sit 90° anticlockwise of the measured
  12, within 8°), the 20 px minimum width, the resize check and the hand checks.
- The provisional levels: CHECK at 0.10 of the width or 2.0°, STRONG at 0.20 or 3.5°.
- The analyzer code moved into a shared helper (`GmtHumanQcAnalyzerV2.measureBaton`).

**What the app shows:**
- A "9 baton" line in the summary and a "HUMAN 9-MARKER QC" section in the report.
- A 9 outline on the overlay and a third close-up panel.
- The bottom line now says "nothing flagged at 12, 6 or 9" when all three pass.

The 3 is left out on purpose, because the date window sits there.
